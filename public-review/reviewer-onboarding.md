# Reviewer Onboarding

**Objective:** make it easy for a qualified person to become a CROA reviewer, and clear what that commitment is.

**Audience:** practitioners and researchers who want to do more than comment occasionally.

---

## What a reviewer is

A reviewer is someone who has agreed to apply their expertise to CROA on a recurring basis: reviewing RFCs and findings in their area, weighing in during Final Comment Periods, and helping maintain the quality bar. Reviewers are **advisory** during Public Review and gain binding votes when the Technical Steering Committee is formed (`GOVERNANCE.md` §5).

You are a good fit if you have real experience in one or more of: enterprise/solution architecture, AI/agentic systems engineering, security architecture, AI governance or risk, distributed systems, or relevant academic research.

You do **not** need to be a CROA supporter. Reviewers who are skeptical of the framework are valuable precisely because they are skeptical.

## What we ask

- **Time:** a few hours per month, self-paced. No fixed quota.
- **Engagement in your lane:** review items labeled in your `area:` of expertise; you are not expected to review everything.
- **Reasoned positions:** support views with argument or evidence; cite the relevant Part/section or a finding.
- **Good faith and the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md):** challenge ideas hard, treat people well.

## Conflict of interest & neutrality

CROA's credibility depends on visible neutrality. Reviewers:

- **Disclose affiliations** relevant to CROA (employer, products, consulting interests) on joining; these are recorded in the reviewers list.
- **Recuse** from decisions where they have a material commercial interest in the outcome.
- Do not use the reviewer role to advantage a specific product or implementation. No implementation receives privileged standing (`GOVERNANCE.md` §2).

Multiple reviewers from the same organization are welcome, but the review board is composed to avoid any single employer holding disproportionate influence.

## How to join

1. Read [`docs/why-croa.md`](../docs/why-croa.md), the [one-page architecture overview](../docs/architecture-overview.md), and the [open research questions](research-questions.md).
2. Run the [Quick Start](../docs/quick-start.md) so you've seen the architecture behave.
3. Make one substantive contribution — a comment on a research question, a clarification issue, or a challenge. This is how we (and you) calibrate fit.
4. Open a short **"Reviewer application"** Discussion (template provided) stating your area(s) of expertise, relevant background, affiliations to disclose, and roughly how much time you can give.
5. A maintainer follows up. Admission is based on relevant expertise and a demonstrated willingness to engage constructively — not on seniority or affiliation.

## What you get

- A voice in how CROA evolves, recorded publicly.
- Credit: reviewers are listed in `REVIEWERS.md` and acknowledged in releases.
- Early visibility into a governance approach for agentic AI, and a network of serious practitioners working the same problem.
- A path to the review board and, eventually, the Technical Steering Committee.

## Leaving

Step back any time — tell us, or simply go inactive. We periodically move long-inactive reviewers to an "alumni" list to keep the active roster honest. No hard feelings; re-join whenever.
